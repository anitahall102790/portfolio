Azure Modernization

Modernizing a legacy enterprise application while balancing technical delivery, stakeholder needs, integrations, testing, and ongoing operational priorities.

My Role

Business Analyst · Scrum Master · Deputy PM

Team

4 Engineers · Contractor PM · Federal PMs

Environment

Microsoft Azure · Enterprise Government Systems

Focus

Modernization · Delivery · Stakeholder Management

The Challenge

This wasn't a simple lift-and-shift migration. The existing application needed to be completely rewritten for Azure while continuing to support the people and business processes that depended on it.

The modernization touched multiple systems and departments, with integrations, security requirements, and agency technology constraints influencing what we could build. At the same time, the team remained responsible for ongoing operations, maintenance, help desk requests, and competing delivery timelines.

That meant the challenge wasn't only technical. We needed to keep modernization moving while managing changing priorities, coordinating stakeholders, validating requirements, and making sure decisions made during the rewrite still supported how people actually needed to use the system.

My Role

I sat between the client, the users, and the technical team, helping turn business needs into executable work while keeping delivery moving.

Requirements & Technical Translation

Worked with users and stakeholders to understand business needs, clarify requirements, and translate them into user stories and actionable work for the engineering team.

Agile Delivery

Led Scrum ceremonies, managed and refined the backlog in Azure DevOps, coordinated priorities, and helped the team navigate changing work across modernization, O&M, and support requests.

Testing & Release Readiness

Coordinated SIT and UAT, developed test plans and scripts, documented release changes, and worked across teams to make sure new functionality was ready for implementation.

Stakeholder & Delivery Coordination

Partnered with contractor and federal project leadership, engineers, users, and dependent teams to communicate status, surface risks and dependencies, and keep decisions moving.

The Approach

With modernization happening alongside ongoing operations, the work required just as much coordination and prioritization as it did technical planning.

Start with how the system was actually used

Before translating functionality into a new environment, I worked with users and stakeholders to understand existing workflows, pain points, dependencies, and the business processes the application needed to continue supporting.

Turn complexity into manageable work

Requirements were broken into actionable stories and organized through the backlog so the team could balance modernization work with operational requests, defects, and changing priorities.

Surface constraints early

Security requirements, agency policies, integrations, and dependencies could change what was technically possible. I worked across stakeholders and the engineering team to identify those constraints early enough to adjust the solution rather than discovering them at release.

Validate before calling it done

SIT and UAT were treated as part of delivery rather than a final checkbox. Test planning and stakeholder validation helped confirm that the rewritten functionality worked technically and still supported the real-world workflows it was replacing.

Key Decision

When the preferred solution wasn't an option.

One requirement called for the application to send communications using alias email addresses. Technically, the approach made sense. In our environment, it wasn't allowed.

Agency Exchange rules prevented the application from using the proposed aliases. Rather than allowing that constraint to stall the requirement, I worked with the technical team and stakeholders to understand what the users actually needed from the feature.

The real need wasn't the alias itself. Users needed communications to come from a recognizable, shared identity instead of an individual account.

We pivoted to a distribution email solution that worked within the agency's constraints while still meeting the underlying business need.

The takeaway Solve for the need, not the original solution.

Outcome & Impact

The modernization delivered a working Azure-based solution while preserving the business workflows, integrations, and operational needs the organization depended on.

Modernized the application

The legacy system was rewritten for Azure, moving the application toward a modern cloud environment rather than continuing to build on aging infrastructure.

Maintained continuity during change

Modernization progressed while the team continued supporting ongoing operations, maintenance, help desk needs, and stakeholder requests.

Navigated enterprise constraints

Security requirements, agency policies, and integration dependencies were incorporated into delivery decisions without losing sight of the underlying user and business needs.

Created a foundation for continued improvement

The new solution provided a more modern technical foundation for ongoing support, enhancement, and future delivery.

What I Took Forward

The best solution isn't always the one you started with.

This project reinforced something that has shaped how I approach technical work ever since: understanding the problem matters more than being attached to a particular solution.

Enterprise environments come with constraints. Priorities change, policies limit what's possible, dependencies surface, and sometimes the original requirement simply won't work the way everyone expected. My job isn't to eliminate that complexity. It's to help the team navigate it without losing sight of what we're actually trying to accomplish.

That's the mindset I carry into new work today: stay curious, make the problem clear, bring the right people into the conversation, and be willing to change the path when the evidence tells us to.
